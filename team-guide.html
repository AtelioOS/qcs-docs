Quality Coffee Systems OS — how it works, and how to work it

A guide for the team: which app to open, what each flow looks like end to end, and what the system does on its own.

The one idea to hold on to: this is one system wearing several faces. A customer, a machine, a work order and an invoice are the same records whichever app you open. You are never copying data between apps — you are looking at the same information from the angle that suits the job in front of you. That is why a serial number typed into Search finds the machine, its work orders and its invoices all at once.

Control CentreAdmin only. People, access, and the Microsoft email connection.atelio-control-center.vercel.app
If you only remember one shortcut: the Search page in the ERP is the Ctrl-F of the whole business. Type a company name, invoice number, PO number, tracking number or machine serial and it searches everything at once. Every result is clickable, and results that live in another app open it for you — so you never need to remember which app a record “belongs” to.

The five everyday flows

1 · A machine comes in for repairFront counter to paid invoice — the most common path.
1
Front counter · Jura Intake tablet
Check the machine in

Customer and machine go in on the tablet, a barcode label prints and goes on the machine. This creates the work order — nobody types it again later.

2
Service & Dispatch
It appears on the bench list

Move it along as the work happens: On Bench → Waiting on Parts → Parts Received → Waiting on Approval → Approved → Completed. The status is how everyone else knows where it is, so keeping it current is the single most useful habit in the system.

3
Technician
Record what you found and did

Assessment, work performed, recommendation, call-log notes. These become the invoice the customer reads — what you write here is what they see.

4
Office
Quote if approval is needed

Put the figure on the work order, set Waiting on Approval. When they say yes, move to Approved.

5
Office
Issue and send the invoice

Add billing lines, press Issue Invoice — it numbers itself and pulls in everything from step 3. Print it, or press Email invoice.

6
Office
Record the payment

Cash, card or e-transfer. The balance updates and they drop off the unpaid list.

Which calendar? The OS reads and writes info@qualitycoffeesystems.ca’s calendar — the company one, not your personal Outlook. If you are checking whether a job made it across, open that mailbox’s calendar or you will see nothing and think it is broken.
2 · An on-site callWhere the calendar and route map earn their keep.
1
Office
Create the work order as RO

RI comes to the bench; RO means we go to them.

2
Dispatch
Date and technician

The moment it has a date it is on the Calendar and on the company Outlook calendar. Change the date later and the Outlook entry moves with it.

3
Route Map
Put the day in driving order

Drag the stop list into the order the technician will drive, then Save route order.

Two honest limits: the line between stops is your running order, not a routed drive — there is no turn-by-turn engine behind it. And a stop only gets a pin once its customer address has been located; the screen tells you which have not been.

4
Technician · Field
Work the list

The Field view is the phone-sized version: today's jobs, customer details, notes and photos from site.

5
Office
Invoice as in flow 1

Field technicians do not take payment on site — the office records it afterwards.

3 · Selling a machineQuote to order, and where the pipeline comes from.
1
Sales · Quote Builder
Build the quote

Machines from the catalogue or typed in freehand. Each line takes its own discount — percentage or flat dollars, whichever way it was negotiated.

2
Sales
Save it

It gets a quote number and, if the customer matches one on file, creates a Pipeline opportunity automatically. That is where the pipeline comes from — you never maintain it by hand.

3
Sales
Email it

Goes from the company mailbox with the spec sheets attached, marks itself sent, and moves the deal along.

4
Sales
Mark the outcome

Accepted or Declined. Accepted closes the opportunity as won. Keeping this honest is what makes the reports worth reading.

4 · Preventative maintenanceSet it up once per machine; the OS does the rest.
1
PM tab
Create the plan

Customer, machine, interval. The next due date calculates itself.

2
Automatic
The work order appears when due

Overnight the OS creates the PM work order and emails the team a summary. Nobody watches a list of dates.

3
Automatic, if switched on
The customer hears first

An email a set number of days beforehand, in your wording. Off until someone turns it on.

4
Technician
Finish with “Finished PM”

That status is what rolls the plan forward. Any other status leaves the plan where it was.

5 · Buying partsPurchase order to stock on the shelf.

ERP · Purchasing

Supplier and lines. It takes the next number in sequence, continuing the run from QuickBooks.

2
ERP

Same layout as the printed version. Sending marks the PO sent.

3
ERP
Receive it

Fully or partially. Stock moves on lines matching a catalogue item, and work orders waiting on those parts can move on.

Learning it: walk the whole flow together

The best way to learn this system is to put one imaginary customer all the way through it. Do this as a group, on real screens, with someone driving and everyone else watching. It takes about twenty minutes and it will surface more questions than any amount of reading.

Use an obviously fake name — something like “Test Cafe (DELETE ME)” — so nobody mistakes it for a real account later, and so it is easy to find and remove afterwards.

The full loop: new customer to paid invoice Roughly twenty minutes, all in one sitting.
1
ERP · Clients
Create the customer

Name it “Test Cafe (DELETE ME)”. Give it a real street address — a real one matters, because step 6 needs to find it on a map. Add an email address you can actually receive mail at, because steps 8 and 9 will send to it.

2
Search
Find them again

Go to Search and type part of the name. They should come up, and clicking the row should open their record. This is the habit worth building: search before you create.

3
Service & Dispatch · Machines
Add a machine

Give it a serial number, make and model. Now search that serial — notice the result is badged MACHINES ↗ and opens the Machines app on that unit. That is the cross-app link working.

4
Service & Dispatch
Raise a work order

Type RO (we go to them), pick the machine, write a realistic issue. Watch it appear on the Pending WO list at status New WO.

5
Dispatch
Schedule it

Give it today's date and a technician. Then check two places: the Calendar tab, and info@'s Outlook calendar. It should be in both.

6
Route Map
See it on the map

Open Route Map, set the date to today. Your job should appear as a numbered pin with the customer's address underneath. Add a second test job at a different address and drag the two into a different order — the map line follows. Press Save route order.

If your stop shows with no pin, that customer's address has not been located yet. Press Locate next 50 in the orange banner and give it a couple of minutes.

Expect this to take a while to fill in, and expect some customers never to get a pin. The addresses came across from QuickBooks as one freeform field, and plenty of them hold an attention line, a company name or a phone number alongside the street — a few hold only a phone number. Those are flagged rather than guessed at, and the fix is tidying the address on the customer record, not on the map.

7
Technician view
Do the work

Move the status along — On Bench, then fill in assessment and what you did, then Completed. Write these fields properly even in the test: you are about to see them turn into a customer-facing document.

8
Office
Invoice it and email it

Add a couple of billing lines, press Issue Invoice, then Email invoice to the address from step 1. Open what arrives and read it as if you were the customer. Does it look like something QCS would send?

9
Sales
Quote them something, and email that too

In the Quote Builder, build a quote for the same customer with a machine from the catalogue. Save it, then press Email…. Check the spec sheets came attached. Then mark it Accepted and look at the Pipeline — the deal should have moved to won on its own.

10
Everyone
Tidy up, and write down what felt wrong

Void the test work orders and delete the test customer. Then, while it is fresh, write down every moment where someone said “wait, where is…?” or “that is not how we do it”. That list is the most valuable thing to come out of the session.

Habits that make the system worth having

  1. Keep work-order status current. It is how dispatch, the calendar, the reports and everyone else knows what is happening. A stale status is worse than no system, because people trust it.
  2. Write the assessment and the work performed properly. Those words become the invoice the customer reads and the history the next technician relies on.
  3. Search before you create. Customers, machines and suppliers already exist far more often than you would expect — 2,700 customers and 9,200 machines came across from QuickBooks. A duplicate splits a machine's history in two.
  4. Use Finished PM for PM work. It is the only status that rolls the plan forward.
  5. Mark quotes accepted or declined. The pipeline is only as honest as the last person to touch it.
  6. If something looks wrong, say so. A number that looks off usually means something upstream needs fixing, and it is far cheaper to fix in the first week than the first year.

Setting up the Jura counter station

The iPad check-in desk at Quality Coffee Systems — customer signs their machine in, the work order creates itself, and the barcode label prints on the spot.

⏱ About 30 minutes, once · then it just runs
What you're building: a small station on the front counter. A customer walks in with a machine, taps through four short screens on the iPad, and a label prints. The work order is already in the OS before they've left the shop — no paperwork, no re-typing, and every tech can find that machine later by scanning its label.

Before you start

iPad (A16)Model MD3Y4CL/A · iPadOS 26
MUNBYN RW402BThermal label printer (AirPrint)
Labels 2.25 × 1.25″MUNBYN thermal, direct — no ink
Adesso NuScan 2700RWireless barcode scanner ×2

You'll also need: the shop Wi-Fi password, and a QCS sign-in (the same Microsoft account you use for the OS).

Part 1 — The label printer

1 Load the labels and power up

  1. Press the latch on the side and lift the lid.
  2. Drop the label roll in so the labels feed off the top of the roll, printable side up.
  3. Pull about 10 cm of label out past the tear bar, then close the lid until it clicks.
  4. Hold the power button until the light comes on. Then press Feed once — one blank label should come out and stop cleanly at the gap. If it spits out several or stops mid-label, hold Feed for ~3 seconds to run the auto-calibration.
Label roll orientation — labels come off the top:

2 Put the printer on the shop Wi-Fi

This is the one fiddly part, and you only do it once. The printer needs to be on the same Wi-Fi network as the iPad — if your shop has a separate guest network, put both on the main one.

  1. Install MUNBYN Print from the App Store on the iPad (free).
  2. Turn the printer on and hold its Wi-Fi/pair button until the indicator blinks.
  3. Open the app → Add printer → pick the RW402B when it appears → choose your shop Wi-Fi network and enter the password.
  4. Wait for the light to go solid. The printer now has its own address on your network and will reconnect on its own after a power cut.
Check this before moving on: on the iPad, open any photo → Share → Print. The RW402B should be listed as a printer. If it is, AirPrint is working and the OS will be able to print to it. If it isn't, the printer is on a different network than the iPad — redo this step.

Part 2 — The iPad

3 Sign in and open Machine Check-in

  1. Connect the iPad to the shop Wi-Fi.
  2. Open Safari and go to atelio-qcs.vercel.app
  3. Sign in with your QCS Microsoft account, exactly like on a computer.
  4. On the Control Centre, tap the Jura Intake tile. (You can also go straight there: atelio-service.vercel.app/#/intake)
What the check-in screen looks like — this is what the customer sees:

4 Add it to the Home Screen

This gives you a proper app icon and hides Safari's address bar — so it looks like a purpose-built kiosk, not a web page a customer might wander off from.

  1. With the check-in page open, tap the Share icon (the square with the arrow, top of Safari).
  2. Scroll down → Add to Home Screen → name it Check-in → Add.
  3. Close Safari. From now on, open the station from that icon.

5 Lock it to that one screen (Guided Access)

Optional but strongly recommended for a counter that customers touch. It pins the iPad to the check-in app — nobody can wander into email, settings or the App Store, and it takes a passcode to get out.

  1. Settings → Accessibility → Guided Access → turn it on.
  2. Tap Passcode Settings → Set Guided Access Passcode and choose a code the counter staff know. Write it somewhere the team can find it.
  3. Open the Check-in app, then triple-click the top button → Start.
  4. To unlock at end of day: triple-click again and enter the passcode.
Also worth setting: Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock → Never while the iPad is plugged in at the counter, so the screen is always awake and ready.

Part 3 — First real test

6 Run one machine through, start to finish

Do this before the first customer — with a real machine on the bench, or with your own name as a test.

  1. On the iPad, search a customer (or add a new one) and tap them.
  2. Pick the machine, or register a new one with its serial number.
  3. Type the issue in plain words — "not heating", "grinder jammed" — and choose Repair In (bench) for a drop-off.
  4. Tap through to the finish. You'll see the label preview on the last screen.
  5. Tap Print label → pick the RW402B → Print.
What comes out — 2.25 × 1.25″, customer, date, barcode, and the work-order code:
  1. Stick the label on the machine (or on the tag you hang off it).
  2. On any computer in the shop, open Service & Dispatch → Pending WO — your new work order is already sitting there.
If the label prints tiny, cut off, or on two labels: in the iPad print dialog, check the paper size is the 2.25 × 1.25″ label stock and scaling is 100% (not "Fit to page"). Set it once and iPadOS remembers it.

Part 4 — The barcode scanners

7 Pair each Adesso NuScan 2700R

These are wireless scanners with a small USB receiver. They behave exactly like a keyboard — no drivers, no software to install.

  1. Plug the USB receiver into the computer where techs look up jobs (the bench PC and the parts desk are the two obvious spots — you have two scanners).
  2. Turn the scanner on. It should pair with its own receiver automatically; if not, scan the pairing barcode printed in the scanner's quick-start card.
  3. Test it: open a blank Notepad/TextEdit window and scan any barcode — the numbers should type themselves out. That's all the OS needs.

8 Scan a work order open

This is the part techs will use fifty times a day, and there's nothing to learn.

  1. On the computer, open the OS anywhere in Service & Dispatch — dashboard, work-order list, anywhere.
  2. Scan the label on a machine.
  3. That machine's work order opens on screen. No clicking, no searching.
It works from any screen in the service desk — you don't have to be in a search box or on a particular page. Scan and the job appears.

Everyday use, once it's set up

SituationWhat to do
Customer drops a machine offHand them the iPad. Four screens, label prints, done. Stick the label on the machine.
Customer isn't sure of their detailsAny staff member can run the check-in for them — it's the same screens.
Something looks off mid-check-inThe iPad shows a "Please see a team member" screen. A staff member approves it or voids it — nothing broken gets created.
Tech picks up a machine from the benchScan the label. The work order opens. Update status, add notes, log parts.
Machine goes back to the customerComplete the work order and issue the invoice as normal — the label stays on it as its ID.

If something goes wrong

ProblemFix
Printer doesn't appear when printingPrinter and iPad are on different Wi-Fi networks. Put both on the main shop network (step 2).
Labels come out blankThe roll is in upside down — thermal labels only print on one side. Flip the roll.
Printing skips or cuts labels in halfHold Feed ~3 seconds to re-calibrate to the label gap.
Scanning does nothingTest in Notepad first. If nothing types, it's the scanner/receiver pairing, not the OS. If it types, make sure the OS window is the one in focus.
iPad asks to sign in againNormal after a long idle period. Sign in once; it stays signed in.
Customer taps out of the appTurn on Guided Access (step 5) — it stops that happening at all.

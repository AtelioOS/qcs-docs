Card payments: Stripe vs the PayPal-only setup

Prepared by Viviana · Atelio, a Loom Tech platformSeptember 10, 2026

The question from the Sept 9 review: online orders are confirmed today by checking PayPal by hand. The OS already has Stripe payment links built in (dark until the account is connected). This one-pager compares the two on the things that decide it — chargeback and fraud protection, what staff have to do, and how the money lands in the books. It is a comparison, not a pitch: the honest answer may be "both."

How each one works here

Today (PayPal only): the customer pays on PayPal; someone signs into PayPal, finds the payment, and confirms the order by hand. The OS never sees the payment happen — a person carries the fact across. With Stripe connected: staff click "Pay link" on an invoice; the customer types their card on Stripe's page (card numbers never touch the OS — that is a hard rule of the build); the webhook stamps the invoice paid the moment the charge succeeds, and the ledger posts it on the next "Post from activity." No inbox-checking, no marking-paid by hand.

The comparison

What mattersStripe (built into the OS)PayPal as used today
Fraud screening Automatic. Every charge is scored by Stripe Radar (machine-learning fraud screening across Stripe's whole network) before it succeeds; risky cards are blocked or challenged with 3-D Secure without anyone at QCS doing anything. Present but opaque. PayPal screens too, but the merchant controls and risk detail are thin on a standard business account — you mostly see the outcome, not the risk.
Chargebacks — who wins Evidence flows. Disputes arrive with a deadline and a structured evidence form; the OS holds the invoice, the order, the delivery/tracking fields and the customer history in one place, so assembling evidence is minutes. 3-D Secure-authenticated charges generally shift fraud liability to the card issuer. Seller protection has terms. PayPal Seller Protection can cover "unauthorized transaction" and "item not received" claims — but only when its conditions are met (eligible item type, proof of delivery to the address on file, etc.). "Not as described" disputes are commonly decided buyer-friendly.
Dispute fees Per-dispute fee (returned if you win, on current published terms — verify at signing). Per-dispute fee on card-funded claims; PayPal's own claim process is separate from the card networks'.
Holds & frozen funds Possible for new accounts — Stripe can hold rolling reserves while it learns the business; equipment-sized tickets ($5–30k) are exactly what triggers a look. Worth pre-empting: tell Stripe the average ticket size when connecting. The famous risk. PayPal is known for 21-day holds and account-level freezes on unusual activity, and a five-figure machine sale IS unusual activity for a new pattern. Money in limbo is the operational story people write about.
Staff work per payment None. Webhook stamps the invoice paid; the red tape disappears. Refunds happen from the invoice (built this week) and reconcile themselves. Manual. Someone checks PayPal, matches the payment to the order, marks it confirmed, and re-keys the amount for the books. Every manual copy is a chance to be wrong.
Reconciliation for Marie Built. Payments land in Stripe clearing until the payout hits the bank; Bank rec matches the payout line. The paper trail is complete without anyone typing. By hand. PayPal balance → transfer → bank line, matched manually; fees have to be split out of the gross by hand.
Fees (order of magnitude) Standard published card rate around 2.9% + 30¢ per domestic online charge (more for international/currency conversion). Large tickets can justify asking Stripe for custom pricing. Comparable headline rate for card-funded PayPal payments — the two are close enough that fees should not decide this.
What the customer sees A clean card page with QCS's name — no account needed. Familiar to many buyers; some businesses simply prefer paying through their PayPal account.
The honest recommendation: Stripe as the rail, PayPal as an option. Connect Stripe and let the payment-link flow do the work — automated fraud screening, structured disputes, hands-free reconciliation. Keep accepting PayPal for the customers who insist on it, recorded in one click with the "Record payment → PayPal" button that shipped Sept 8, so it still lands in the books properly. What ends either way is the manual checking: nobody's morning should start by signing into PayPal to see who paid.
Numbers to verify before signing anything. Exact fees, dispute charges, reserve policy and payout timing are set by each provider's current terms and can be negotiated at QCS's volume — treat the figures above as the published order of magnitude, and confirm current pricing on both accounts before Part 2 concludes.
